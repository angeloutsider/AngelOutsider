Portrait of Me at 23

10/28/2026
By Raine K. Torres

I turned 22 exactly one year ago today at Yoyogi Milk Hall, a Showa-themed bar and eatery with colorful soda floats and twinkling walls lined with the fresh faced, bright eyed starlets of my mother’s adolescence.

I too was fresh faced and bright eyed then, having moved to Japan 21 days prior to pursue a music opportunity, but the clarity of my eyes was not from the jubilation of youth but from the tears that flowed the second the clock struck midnight, signifying that it was my birthday.

I was crying a lot those days, at any and everything: photos of my mom and grandma when they were younger, a stock video of downtown Los Angeles in the background of “Night Changes” at karaoke, my dog Bubbles on facetime. I would later realize that this was primarily the result of stopping birth control but, at the time, I knew it to be solely caused by home sickness.

This was not some common type of homesickness though––there was mystery to it, for I was no stranger to Japan and I lived with my grandma in the house that I spent a good portion of my life in. I had friends, a home, and only planned to be in Japan for a year. I couldn’t understand why the tears flowed so continuously.

Following this was 7 months, 13 shows, 3 jobs, and 2 haircuts before I returned back to LA to record with a different company than the one I initially moved to Japan for. Somewhere in that time and space, I became convinced I had achieved some sort of enlightenment, for I had developed what I called a gift mentality. I am a frequently anxious person yet, for the first time ever, I felt so strongly and pervasively that everything was out of my hands and I was okay with that (#stoicism).

I decided that every single thing, from the air in my lungs to the family I had left, was actually a gift and I should be grateful for it. I no longer worried about death since I realized, I should actually just be grateful that I was alive. This freed me from shackles in a way I didn’t think possible and when I returned to LA, I could drive without my hands hurting from clutching the wheel so tight and I was no longer plagued with constant visions of the million ways I could crash.

Every day was sunshine and every moment felt like it needed to be bottled, just so that I could remember how amazing it was to feel so much love and friendship and gratitude all the time.

And then I returned to Japan and the feeling was gone.

For the life of me I couldn’t remember how I achieved the gift mindset. Everything felt rotten, including myself. I didn’t want to see anyone, I didn’t want to go anywhere, I didn’t want to do anything. The music I had recorded in LA was in limbo, being in Japan meant I couldn’t apply for the museum jobs I wanted in LA, and my skin was terrible (I still can’t figure out why this is since the water is supposed to be better here).

This time I couldn’t blame it on birth control. I just felt that given the political and environmental state of the world and the rapid proliferation of AI and the stupid ass robot olympics, it all just felt so hopeless and I would soon be jobless and a burden to my family.

I felt I lacked autonomy on every front, that I was in some sort of purgatory simply wasting away as things happened around me. And even if I were to take some action, what would it all be for? In August, I hung out with no one and barely left the house. In September, I decided I needed to move out of my grandma’s house.

It is now October and I am now turning 23 in a lovely sharehouse I found in Tokyo. I teach English online most days and have recording sessions or rehearsals or cafe dates with friends on others. I do YouTube pilates on the patio to let the sun kiss my face whenever I have time and I make myself grand breakfasts in stolen moments between classes. The feelings of existentialism have not left me but I do feel some autonomy returned to me as things have finally started moving, (including me!)

The conclusion I came to is that, in these most darkest of times, I must be in pursuit of love and of knowledge, for those are the things that make us human. While it may seem that greater beings are in possession of infinitely more knowledge, we must never underestimate the value of our own pursuit of it. The curiosity, the desire to know more, to strive for beyond what is presented to us. And to love, it is what defines our humanity and brings us closer to the heavens.

And so at 23, I will try to read more and love more. I’m currently reading Communion: The Female Search for Love by bell hooks and Woman of Genius by Simone de Beauvoir. I’m also trying to be a better cook. I Facetime my friends and mom often and remind myself of the love I have both in Tokyo and in LA. I’m taking Spanish classes online. Sometimes my friends consult me about love, which makes me happy given that they trust my opinion despite my lack of resume.

The more I think about it, 22 was a good age for me. I watched The Fall (2006) by Tarsem, which is now my favorite of all time, closely followed by Uptown Girls (2003). I learned that my favorite flower to be given is (lilies) and that I love figs but raspberries will always be my favorite fruit. I started taking Traditional Chinese Medicine and remembered how much I love the ocean in California. I went to Portugal and England for the first time, I learned what it feels like to have a strong and prominent father figure in my life, I learned that friendships can grow and change and take place a million miles away yet still feel so beautiful and warm and kiss me tenderly atop the head whenever I think about it. My favorite song of all time is still Perfect Day by Lou Reed.

I would like to try falling in love this year. I’ve still never been in love and would like to put all which I have ruminated and advised on into practice. It’s not very easy for me given that I historically do not give in easily to feelings however I think it’s important to try. Regardless of if I'm successful in love on the front of romance, at 23, I hope to continually pursue love in everything. In every relationship, in every breath, in every moment, I want to try to find the love in it because that is how you find God.

For my birthday, I made myself a ginormous breakfast and then walked to Setagaya museum and then had a very lovely party at my new house with many beautiful friends where I ate a delicious cake that Ohana made and Ken bought me many vinyls and Lisa came early and ate karaage and drank wine with me as an anthology of John Coltrane’s live performances played on the projector.

My other goals this year are to be able to do multiple pushups with impeccable form, to be able to cook a meal that pleases all the senses for a party of more than one, to reduce my screen time and sleep more and earlier, to feel strong and beautiful in my mind, body, spirit, and soul, to finish and put out the record and see it resonate, and to let my heart and arms be open without anyone giving me reason to stop.

God willing, I will have many years on this earth and I hope to continually find love and beauty and joy and light even in the darkest of times.
